**(chat):** 
| Parameter | Symbol | Definition / Physical Relationship | Typical Value Range |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Magnification** | $M_{total}$ | Product of objective ($M_{obj}$) and eyepiece ($M_{oc}$) magnifications:<br>$$M_{total} = M_{obj} \times M_{oc}$$ | $40\times$ to $1000\times$ |
| **Numerical Aperture** | $\text{NA}$ | Measures the light-gathering capacity of the objective:<br>$$\text{NA} = n \sin(\alpha)$$<br>*(where $n$ is the refractive index of the medium, and $\alpha$ is the half-angle of the maximum cone of light)* | $0.10$ (air, $n=1.0$) to $1.40$ (oil immersion, $n=1.51$) |
| **Lateral Resolution** | $r_{xy}$ or $d$ | Minimum resolvable distance between two points (Abbe limit / Rayleigh criterion):<br>$$r_{xy} = \frac{0.61 \lambda}{\text{NA}}$$<br>*(where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light)* | $200\text{ nm} - 250\text{ nm}$ (for visible light) |
| **Axial Resolution** | $r_z$ | Minimum resolvable depth along the optical axis:<br>$$r_z = \frac{2 \lambda \cdot n}{\text{NA}^2}$$ | $500\text{ nm} - 800\text{ nm}$ |
| **Depth of Field** | $\text{DOF}$ | The axial range where the image remains acceptably sharp:<br>$$\text{DOF} \approx \frac{\lambda \cdot n}{\text{NA}^2} + \frac{n}{M \cdot \text{NA}} e$$<br>*(where $e$ is the detector pixel size or eye resolution limit)* | $0.2\,\mu\text{m}$ (high $\text{NA}$) to $50\,\mu\text{m}$ (low $\text{NA}$) |
| **Field of View** | $\text{FOV}$ | The physical diameter of the region observed on the specimen:<br>$$\text{FOV} = \frac{\text{FN}}{M_{obj}}$$<br>*(where $\text{FN}$ is the Field Number of the eyepiece)* | $0.2\text{ mm}$ (at $1000\times$) to $5.0\text{ mm}$ (at $40\times$) |
| **Working Distance** | $\text{WD}$ | Physical distance from the front lens element to the coverslip/specimen surface when in focus. | $0.1\text{ mm}$ (high $\text{NA}$) to $40\text{ mm}$ (low $\text{NA}$ / long-working-distance lenses) |
| **Wavelength** | $\lambda$ | The electromagnetic wavelength of the illumination source. | $400\text{ nm} - 700\text{ nm}$ (mean $\lambda \approx 550\text{ nm}$ for white light) |
